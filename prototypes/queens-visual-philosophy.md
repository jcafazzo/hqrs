# Causal Tricolour

Causal Tricolour treats colour as an explanatory system. Queen’s Blue is the field in which complex work becomes visible; it holds attention without noise and gives the room a consistent institutional horizon. Gold is the signal: a due time, a moment of recovery, an invitation to look. Red marks consequence or pressure sparingly, never as decoration. White is evidence space. The three colours are calibrated with painstaking attention so that their hierarchy is read before it is consciously noticed.

Space behaves like stagecraft. A thesis is allowed to become monumental, while source material is allowed to occupy almost the entire field. Ordinary teaching slides alternate between deep-blue theatre and quiet white evidence planes, creating rhythm without visual novelty for its own sake. Every margin, crop, and alignment should feel meticulously crafted by someone at the top of the field; the composition must remain legible from the last row, not merely elegant on a laptop.

Photography carries circumstances rather than supplying atmosphere. Clinical environments, tools, hands, corridors, and teams appear with documentary specificity, leaving deliberate negative space for a single thought. Images should reveal the work system around an action: what was available, what competed for attention, and where detection or recovery occurred. The result should feel editorial and authentic, with master-level restraint rather than stock-photo optimism.

Evidence receives a separate visual grammar. When a page or figure matters, it is first contextualized and then repeated on a dedicated observation slide, enlarged until its internal relationships can be read across the room. A large cue and a generous attribution rail are the only companions. This treatment is the product of laborious refinement: the source is not a thumbnail inside a decorated frame but the subject of the slide.

Typography is direct, large, and disciplined. Open Sans or the platform system sans carries most of the lecture; weight, scale, and line breaks create drama without resorting to effects. Words are few enough to function as visual objects. The Queen’s mark is used intact with clear space, while the tricolour rail makes the identity pervasive even when the mark appropriately disappears. The final system should look as if every transition, crop, and typographic interval has been revisited countless times.
