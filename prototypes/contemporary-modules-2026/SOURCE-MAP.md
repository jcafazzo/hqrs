# Contemporary module library: source and claim map

Build date: 19 July 2026
Scope: eight independent ten-slide teaching modules
Teaching claim: use error, not user error

This map travels with the modules when slides are copied into another presentation. It distinguishes event facts, measured evidence, source interpretation, teaching inference, and design proposal. A visually persuasive slide never expands what its source established.

## Source hierarchy

1. Official incident bulletin, regulator, government guidance, or national safety investigation for event and policy facts.
2. Peer-reviewed primary research for measured effects and associations.
3. Current programme reports for quality-improvement submissions and operational context.
4. Generated images only for observation, analogy, and transfer. They are not evidence.

## 1. Pulse oximetry

### Primary sources

- Sjoding MW, Dickson RP, Iwashyna TJ, Gay SE, Valley TS. [Racial Bias in Pulse Oximetry Measurement](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc2029240). *New England Journal of Medicine*. 2020;383:2477-2478.
- Health Canada. [Pulse oximeters: For health care providers](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-medical-devices/pulse-oximeters/health-care-providers.html). Page date 30 December 2022.
- US Food and Drug Administration. [Pulse Oximeters for Medical Purposes: Non-Clinical and Clinical Performance Testing, Labeling, and Premarket Submission Recommendations](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/pulse-oximeters-medical-purposes-non-clinical-and-clinical-performance-testing-labeling-and). Draft guidance, January 2025.

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| In the Sjoding retrospective analysis, occult hypoxemia occurred in 11.7% of measurements from Black patients and 3.6% from White patients in one cohort. | Measured association | Measurement-level retrospective result with source definition and denominator. Do not call it a universal device error rate or a causal mechanism. |
| A displayed SpO2 can disagree with arterial oxygen saturation. | Measurement principle | Pulse oximetry remains clinically useful. Interpret trends, symptoms, perfusion, use conditions, and other clinical information. |
| Health Canada describes typical regulated-device accuracy around 2% to 4% compared with arterial blood gas. | Government guidance | Typical is not a guarantee for every device, person, saturation, or condition. |
| The January 2025 FDA document proposes performance and study-diversity requirements. | Draft regulator guidance | Label it draft, non-binding, and not for implementation. Recheck status before reuse. |

### Never say

- Pulse oximeters are useless.
- Every device has the same disparity.
- Race is a direct optical mechanism.
- A draft FDA recommendation is already a binding requirement.

## 2. Corridor care

### Primary sources

- Health Services Safety Investigations Body. [Patient care in temporary care environments](https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/patient-care-in-temporary-care-environments/investigation-report/). Published 8 January 2026.
- Health Services Safety Investigations Body. [Medication not given: administration of time critical medication in the emergency department](https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/medication-related-harm/investigation-report/). Published 5 December 2024.
- Trainee Emergency Research Network. [Understanding corridor and escalation area care in 165 UK emergency departments](https://doi.org/10.1136/emermed-2025-215301). *Emergency Medicine Journal*. 2026;43:72-80.
- Canadian Institute for Health Information. [NACRS emergency department visits and lengths of stay](https://www.cihi.ca/en/nacrs-emergency-department-visits-and-lengths-of-stay). Complete-year data April 2024 to March 2025.

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| One investigated patient spent 52 hours in the emergency department, including 44 in a corridor. | Event fact | Same HSSIB event used in the Parkinson's module. It is not an independent corridor case. |
| Of 18 capsule-level scheduled doses, 7 were not given, 3 were late, and 8 were potentially on time. | Event reconstruction | Preserve HSSIB record uncertainty. Do not infer that the corridor or these doses caused deterioration or death. |
| Across five March 2025 snapshots in 165 UK emergency departments, 17.7% of patient observations were in escalation areas. | Cross-sectional point prevalence | Snapshot observations, not annual incidence, duration, or a patient-level causal outcome study. |
| CIHI reported more than 16.1 million unscheduled visits and a 48.5-hour 90th percentile for admitted-patient ED visits in 2024-25. | Canadian flow context | CIHI measured visit duration, not corridor location. Refresh when 2025-26 full-year data are released. |
| Call bells, sockets, monitoring, privacy, and assigned ownership can reduce immediate risk. | Investigation-informed design inference | Harm reduction is not evidence that corridor care is acceptable or that one adaptation is validated. |

### Never say

- Corridor care caused the investigated patient's death.
- Every long Canadian ED visit happened in a corridor.
- Adding infrastructure solves hospital flow.

## 3. Fatigue

### Primary sources

- US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health. [Fatigue and Work](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/fatigue/about/index.html). Updated 3 March 2026.
- Health Services Safety Investigations Body. [The impact of staff fatigue on patient safety](https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/the-impact-of-staff-fatigue-on-patient-safety/investigation-report/). Published 24 April 2025.
- Weaver MD, et al. [Impact of work schedules of senior resident physicians on patient and resident physician safety](https://bmjmedicine.bmj.com/content/2/1/e000320). *BMJ Medicine*. 2023;2:e000320.
- Landrigan CP, et al. [Effect on Patient Safety of a Resident Physician Schedule without 24-Hour Shifts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7405505/). *New England Journal of Medicine*. 2020;382:2514-2523.
- Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality. [Fatigue and Sleepiness of Clinicians Due to Hours of Service](https://effectivehealthcare.ahrq.gov/products/fatigue-sleepiness/rapid-research). *Making Healthcare Safer IV*. May 2024.

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| Fatigue can slow reaction time and reduce attention, short-term memory, and judgment. | Authoritative occupational-health summary | These are ordinary human-performance effects, not proof that a particular clinician caused an event. |
| Weaver et al. included 4,826 senior residents and 38,702 monthly reports; more than 60 to 70 hours versus no more than 48 hours was associated with self-reported medical error, OR 2.36. | Prospective observational association | Hours and outcomes were self-reported. Association does not establish causation or a patient count. |
| In ROSTERS, patients per resident increased from 6.7 to 8.8 under the schedule without 24-hour shifts; unadjusted serious-error rates were 79.0 versus 97.1 per 1,000 patient-days. | Randomized crossover result | Site effects and workload matter. Do not say long shifts are safer. The intervention changed several system conditions. |
| Evidence on fatigue interventions has mixed effects and is concentrated in physician trainees and scheduling interventions. | Government rapid review | English-language research since 2013. No universal best policy was established. |
| Fatigue is poorly represented in incident-learning data. | Investigation finding | HSSIB describes limited measurement and reporting. Do not invent a national harm rate. |

### Never say

- Fatigue caused a specific event unless the source established it.
- Shorter shifts increase errors as a general rule.
- Endurance is a reliable safety control.

## 4. Automated dispensing cabinet frameshift

### Primary source

- Institute for Safe Medication Practices Canada. [Emergency Preparedness Mitigates Patient Harm during Failed Deployment of Upgraded Automated Dispensing Cabinets](https://ismpcanada.ca/bulletin/automated-dispensing-cabinets/). *ISMP Canada Safety Bulletin*. Volume 25, Issue 8. Published 31 July 2025.

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| A database-import frameshift misaligned medication fields during an ADC upgrade. | Event fact | The official Figure 1 is a simplified explanatory diagram, not a production screenshot. |
| Nurses detected discrepancies within minutes. | Event fact and resilience | People were a source of detection and recovery capacity. Do not recast them as the cause. |
| More than 300 products and more than 150 transactions were affected. | Event scale | Do not infer that 150 wrong medications were administered. |
| The Code Grey response lasted about six hours and no patient harm was reported. | Event response and outcome | Preserve reported. Absence of reported harm is not proof that the deployment process was intrinsically safe. |

### Never say

- Wrong medication reached a patient.
- The ADC independently caused harm.
- The near miss proves the system was safe.

## 5. Doloral 1 + 1 = 11

### Primary sources

- Institute for Safe Medication Practices Canada. [Sharing Matters! Concerned Reporting Results in a Safer Product Name](https://ismpcanada.ca/bulletin/doloral/). *ISMP Canada Safety Bulletin*. Volume 25, Issue 2, Supplement 1. Published 20 February 2025.
- Health Canada Drug Product Database. [DOLORAL SIROP, DIN 00614491](https://health-products.canada.ca/dpd-bdpp/info?lang=eng&code=6171), 1 mg/mL.
- Health Canada Drug Product Database. [DOLORAL SIROP, DIN 00614505](https://health-products.canada.ca/dpd-bdpp/info?lang=eng&code=11597), 5 mg/mL.
- Health Canada. [Good label and package practices guide for prescription drugs](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/reports-publications/medeffect-canada/good-label-package-practices-guide-prescription-drugs-profile/guidance-document.html).

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| ISMP Canada received several reports of inadvertent 11-fold dosing errors involving Doloral 1, a 1 mg/mL oral morphine syrup. | Incident-report synthesis | Several is not an exact count. The bulletin does not state direction, administered dose, number of incidents, or number of patients. |
| At least one incident involved severe harm. | Reported outcome | Do not invent clinical details or call every event an overdose. |
| Interpretation of DOLORAL 1 beside 1 mg/mL as 11 mg/mL was a key contributing factor. | Source interpretation | A key factor, not necessarily the only condition in every event. |
| The manufacturer, in communication with Health Canada, removed the numeral from both product names. | Reported redesign | The bulletin does not give the full decision or implementation timeline. |
| Current federal entries list DOLORAL SIROP at 1 mg/mL and 5 mg/mL as marketed. | Current regulator record | A current record does not prove every downstream system or package updated simultaneously. |

### Never say

- Eleven overdoses occurred.
- Health Canada recalled or withdrew the drug.
- The official Figure 1 is an authentic production screenshot. It is a re-creation.

## 6. Time-critical Parkinson's medication

### Primary sources

- Health Services Safety Investigations Body. [Medication not given: administration of time critical medication in the emergency department](https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/medication-related-harm/investigation-report/). Published 5 December 2024.
- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. [Quality statement 4: Levodopa in hospital or a care home](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs164/chapter/quality-statement-4-levodopa-in-hospital-or-a-care-home). QS164. Published 9 February 2018.
- Royal College of Emergency Medicine. [Time Critical Medication QIP 2025: National Report](https://rcem.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/RCEM-Time-Critical-Medications-QIP-National-Report-2025.pdf). Published June 2026.

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| The investigated 85-year-old patient had medication scheduled at 08:00, 12:00, 16:00, and 20:00 in two strengths. | Event fact | Same event as the corridor module. This lens foregrounds timing and continuity. |
| The patient spent 52 hours in the ED, including 44 in a corridor; of 18 reconstructed capsule-level doses, 7 were not given, 3 were late, and 8 were potentially on time. | Event reconstruction | Preserve uncertainty and do not infer causation for deterioration or death. |
| RCEM's 2025 QIP included 122 EDs, 18,570 submissions, and 18,067 eligible cases. | Quality-improvement scope | Participating submissions, not a population census or UK-wide prevalence. |
| 47.6% of patients receiving levodopa were identified within 30 minutes; 38.4% of eligible levodopa dose records were administered within 30 minutes of expected time. | QIP performance | Submission and eligibility rules matter. These are not national patient-harm rates. |
| NICE says levodopa should be given within 30 minutes of the individually prescribed time. | Quality standard | Expert-consensus timeframe, not a claim that all Parkinson's medicines share one schedule. |

### Never say

- Corridor conditions or late medication caused death.
- The RCEM result is a UK prevalence estimate.
- Every Parkinson's medicine is equally time-critical.

## 7. Sepsis AI across four health systems

### Primary source

- Wong A, Currey D, Schwinne M, et al. [Multicenter Prospective Validation of an Updated Proprietary Sepsis Prediction Model](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2845595). *JAMA Network Open*. 2026;9(2):e260181. CC BY.

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| The cohort contained 227,091 inpatient encounters across four US health systems; 7,401 met Sepsis-3 criteria. | Prognostic-study result | Four health systems, not four hospitals. Emory included six affiliated hospitals. |
| All sites fine-tuned ESM v2 using local historical inpatient data. | Implementation fact | One product family, not four identical fitted models. |
| Score thresholds 14, 29, 37, and 35 matched 60% encounter sensitivity at the four sites. | Analytic operating point | These were study comparison thresholds, not reported production alert thresholds. |
| Encounter-level AUROC ranged from 0.82 to 0.92 and PPV from 0.13 to 0.26. | Prognostic performance | AUROC summarizes discrimination across thresholds. PPV is reported at each site's analytic operating point selected for 60% encounter sensitivity. Keep site variation and prevalence context attached. |
| At a 12-hour horizon, prediction-level NNE ranged from 21 to 35. | Analytic workload estimate | Not a count of observed bedside interruptions in one common alert workflow. |
| The study did not evaluate mortality or other clinical outcomes. | Limitation | Never imply that it demonstrated clinical benefit or harm. |

### Never say

- The same fitted model was tested in four hospitals.
- 21 to 35 clinicians had to respond to every true case.
- The model reduced mortality.

## 8. ePMA procurement and lifecycle safety

### Primary sources

- Health Services Safety Investigations Body. [Electronic prescribing and medicines administration: procurement and safety learning in acute hospitals](https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/medication-related-harm/fourth-investigation-report/). Published 28 May 2026.
- Klein S, Tsanas A, Coleman J, et al. [A simulation study to quantitatively assess the performance of electronic prescribing systems in English NHS Hospital Trusts](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-86112-w). *Scientific Reports*. 2025;15:2120. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

### Claims and boundaries

| Claim | Classification | Boundary that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| ePRaSE testing involved 68 pharmacists, 45 English NHS trusts, and 13 electronic prescribing systems. | Simulation-study scope | Voluntary self-assessment, with uneven representation of systems. |
| Mean good mitigation was about 50%; the highest trust score was 73%. | Scenario-response result | Not a medication-error, harm, or patient-outcome rate. The maximum is not a validated ceiling. |
| Trusts using the same product could show different performance. | Study result | Local configuration is a plausible contributor, but the study did not measure every sociotechnical difference or establish all causes. |
| ePRaSE had not been externally validated or psychometrically evaluated. | Study limitation | Tool validation remained future work. |
| HSSIB reported 21 commercial manufacturers on the CDHS framework at report writing. | Investigation context | A framework catalogue count is not local safety assurance. Eligibility responses were self-assessed and not assured by the framework. |
| HSSIB made five safety recommendations and three safety observations. | Investigation output | Recommendation is not implementation or effectiveness evidence. Refresh formal-response status before reuse. |

### Never say

- Half of prescriptions were unsafe.
- The best possible system is 73% safe.
- Framework-listed products were proven locally safe.
- There were no digital clinical safety standards.
- HSSIB recommendations have been implemented unless current response evidence supports that claim.

## Cross-module overlap that must remain explicit

| Pair | Relationship | Editorial rule |
|---|---|---|
| Corridor care and Parkinson's medication | Two lenses on the same HSSIB event | Never present them as two independent cases. Use corridor for environment and flow; use Parkinson's for individualized timing and continuity. |
| Doloral and ADC | Both involve medication information relationships | Keep teaching jobs distinct: semantic adjacency and upstream renaming versus corrupted row relationships, deployment, detection, and recovery. |
| Pulse oximetry and Sepsis AI | Both involve thresholds | Distinguish measurement uncertainty from prediction operating points and workflow burden. |
| Fatigue and every incident case | Fatigue is a general work-system condition | Do not insert fatigue as a causal factor into a case whose source did not identify it. |

## Generated-image boundary

Every Image 2 visual is an original teaching image. It may show a plausible setting, object, or work pattern, but it does not depict a reported patient, named hospital, study site, production interface, or source event. Keep the visible truth label, alternative text, and prompt record when copying a slide.
