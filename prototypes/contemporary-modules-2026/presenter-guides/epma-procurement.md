# Presenter guide: ePMA procurement and lifecycle safety

## Audience contract and argument

**Contract:** Treat procurement as the beginning of a local safety lifecycle.

**Argument:** Electronic prescribing safety is produced through local configuration, testing, use, change, feedback, and shared assurance.

**Target length:** 10 to 12 minutes. The module can also support executive, procurement, digital-governance, patient-safety, and implementation audiences.

## Run of show

| Slide | Time | Presenter move | Ask and expected responses | Transition |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1. Same product | 0:50 | Hold the matched hospital scenes and name the local-safeguards contract. | What changes after purchase? Configuration, interfaces, alerts, formularies, workflow, training, hardware, governance, updates. | A national simulation made some of that variation measurable. |
| 2. 68, 45, 13 | 1:05 | Give the sample before the result. | What does about 50% refer to? Mean good mitigation against prescribing-risk scenarios. | The study tested the local system with work. |
| 3. Fictional patient test | 0:55 | Describe the scenario-response method. | Why is this stronger than a feature list? It observes what the configured system does. | A simulation score still has limits. |
| 4. Score boundary | 1:05 | Read the left and right panels as a claim boundary. | What can we conclude? Variation is measurable. What can we not conclude? Patient outcome effect or complete causal explanation. | Procurement sits inside a longer lifecycle. |
| 5. HSSIB roadmap | 0:55 | Project the whole figure. | Locate procurement. What safety work comes before and after it? | Now ask what a framework listing actually assures. |
| 6. 21 manufacturers | 1:00 | State the count and immediately state its limit. | What would local assurance require? Hazard log, safety case, realistic tests, workflows, roles, monitoring, supplier information. | The control structure is wider than buyer and vendor. |
| 7. HSSIB control map | 1:00 | Ask the class to trace one local signal. | Where can feedback stop? Local reporting, informal networks, supplier, national bodies, regulatory classification, weak handoffs. | The report proposed changes to that system. |
| 8. Five and three | 0:55 | Distinguish recommendation from implementation. | What evidence would show uptake? Formal response, delivery plan, compliance evidence, repeated local testing, outcome or process monitoring. | Bring the question back to the next software change. |
| 9. Change review | 1:20 | Give pairs 45 seconds, then take two ten-second reports. | Strong answers name a hazard, realistic test, stop authority, detection plan, rollback, and learning route. | Safety has to survive change. |
| 10. Maintained property | 0:45 | Return to use error, not user error. | What evidence will show this configuration still supports safe work after the next change? | End. |

**Timed rehearsal estimate:** 9:50 including the default 45-second activity and two ten-second reports. Allow up to 11:05 for transitions and brief unplanned discussion.

## Claim and caveat matrix

| Claim used in the module | Source support | Boundary that must travel with it |
|---|---|---|
| 68 pharmacists, 45 English NHS trusts, 13 electronic prescribing systems | Klein et al., 2025 | Voluntary participation in a self-assessment simulation. |
| About 50% mean good mitigation | Klein et al., results | Scenario-response score, not a medication error, harm, or outcome rate. |
| Maximum trust score 73% | Klein et al., discussion | Highest observed score in the sample, not a validated ceiling. |
| Same system can perform differently across trusts | Klein et al., results | Local configuration is a plausible contributor. The study did not measure every sociotechnical difference or establish all causes. |
| ePRaSE lacked external and psychometric validation | Klein et al., limitations | Tool validation was planned future work. Preserve this when using the score. |
| 21 ePMA manufacturers on the CDHS framework | HSSIB, section 3.5.1 | Count at report writing. Framework presence is not proof of local safety. |
| Framework eligibility responses were self-assessed and not assured by the framework | HSSIB, sections 3.5.3 to 3.5.5 | Do not say the products were unregulated or that no digital safety standards existed. |
| Five safety recommendations and three safety observations | HSSIB, executive summary | Recommendations and observations are not implementation or effectiveness evidence. |
| Safety risks can change with configuration, workflow redesign, and upgrades | HSSIB, investigation and consultation | This supports repeat local testing and lifecycle review. It does not prove a specific review method is effective. |

## Facilitation notes

- If someone says staff need more training, ask what configuration, workflow, test, staffing, monitoring, and supplier controls must accompany it.
- If someone treats a procurement framework as certification, distinguish minimum framework requirements from local implementation assurance.
- If someone says there are no safety standards, correct this. HSSIB described legally mandated digital clinical safety and interoperability standards alongside variation in compliance, oversight, enforcement, and ePMA-specific requirements.
- If someone asks which vendor was best in ePRaSE, explain that systems and trusts were anonymized and the tool was designed for self-assessment and improvement, not vendor ranking.

## Reuse and shortening

**Can be shortened:**

- Use only one HSSIB artifact, preferably the roadmap for procurement audiences or the control map for governance audiences.
- Combine slides 2 and 4 verbally if the audience already understands simulation evidence.

**Must remain together:**

- About 50% and the scenario-response boundary.
- The 21-manufacturer count and the statement that framework listing is not local safety assurance.
- HSSIB recommendations and the statement that recommendation does not equal implementation.

**Never separate:**

- ePRaSE metrics from voluntary participation and tool-validation limitations.
- HSSIB Figure 2 or Figure 3 from the visible report attribution.
- Any generated work scene from its hypothetical, not-evidence label.

## Publication and image boundary

- Slides 1, 3, 6, and 9 use original Image 2 teaching images. They are hypothetical and visibly labelled.
- Slides 5 and 7 use HSSIB report figures extracted whole with no content alteration. Preserve credit, source link, and classroom/public-review context. Recheck permission before commercial republication.
- The Scientific Reports article uses CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. This module does not reproduce or adapt its figures. The native HTML evidence display is an independent factual summary with citation.
- If a Scientific Reports figure is added later, use the whole original without crop, recolour, annotation, or rearrangement, and preserve the licence credit.
