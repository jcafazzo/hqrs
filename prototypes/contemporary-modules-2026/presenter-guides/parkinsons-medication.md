# Presenter guide: time-critical Parkinson medication

Status: READY · library acceptance passed 19 July 2026
Entry point: `../parkinsons-medication.html`
Target: 10 to 12 minutes, ten slides

## Audience contract and argument

**Contract:** Follow one schedule across every transition without turning one missed dose into a one-person explanation.

**Argument:** When ownership and information fragmented, the patient and family were asked to supply the integration.

This module and the corridor-care module are different lenses on the same HSSIB investigated event. They are not independent cases. Use the corridor lens for space, flow, infrastructure and capacity. Use this lens for exact timing, regimen continuity, records, ePMA, self-administration and ownership.

## Structured interaction

Divide 44 to 45 learners into six pods of seven or eight:

1. Identify
2. Verify
3. Prescribe
4. Supply
5. Administer or support self-administration
6. Handoff and downtime

Prompt:

> Design one reliable link in the chain. Name the owner, trigger, visible completion signal and fallback when the digital system is unavailable. Then name one way the link could fail while appearing complete.

Expected strong responses include a time-critical medication question at first contact tied to an owned follow-up task, exact individualized times rather than standard rounds, a reconciliation state that preserves conflicts until resolved, pharmacist support, local access to medication, supported self-administration with secure storage and documentation, an actionable ePMA flag, a downtime list of due time-critical medicine, and an overdue-dose view that separates late, not given and patient-administered doses.

Do not accept "educate everyone" as a complete control. Ask who acts, when, how completion becomes visible and what happens when the preferred record or service is unavailable.

## Caveats and forbidden shortcuts

- Do not say missed or late medication caused the patient's death.
- Do not say corridor care caused all late or missed doses.
- Do not use an exact count of self-administered doses. The public report contains record uncertainty and internal inconsistency.
- Do not call 18 separate medication rounds. The denominator counts two strengths as capsule-level doses.
- Do not imply every person with Parkinson disease has the same schedule.
- Do not make 30 minutes a universal biological cliff.
- Do not present self-administration as a blanket solution. It needs assessment, access, storage, documentation and fallback.
- Do not treat family knowledge as a substitute for medication reconciliation.
- Do not say ePMA would automatically have prevented the event.
- Do not describe RCEM QI results as UK population prevalence.
- Do not infer fatigue, negligence or lack of compassion.
- Do not present the corridor and medication modules as independent cases.

## Reuse rules

**Eight-minute cut:** use slides 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10. Narrate the 52-hour and RCEM context in notes only if time permits.

**Must remain together:**

- Slide 2 must retain the expert-consensus caveat.
- Slide 3 must retain the capsule-level denominator and "potentially."
- Slide 4 must retain the non-causation statement.
- Slide 6 must retain the statement that outage was one interacting condition.
- Slide 7 must remain whenever the corridor-care module appears in the same course or deck.
- Slide 8 must retain participating-QI-submission language.

**Never separate:** Do not copy 8 of 18 without uncertainty and denominator context. Do not use the death as a consequence claim. Do not count this and corridor care as two independent incidents. Do not use generated people as depictions of the patient or family.

For public publication, retain source links, source-figure attribution and generated-image truth labels. Classroom educational use does not automatically settle public-web reuse of report figures. If needed, replace embedded report excerpts with a direct source link and a native, source-faithful data display.
