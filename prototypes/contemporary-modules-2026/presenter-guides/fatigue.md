# Presenter guide: Fatigue

## Contract

- **Audience contract:** In eleven minutes, learners will treat fatigue as a foreseeable operating condition and test one system control for the work it may displace.
- **Argument:** Changing human capacity is a system design input. A safer roster must redesign workload, handoffs, recovery, task safeguards and authority, not only shorten the clock.
- **Runtime:** 10:00 with 45 seconds of table work and two ten-second reports. The full five-table report belongs in the twelve-minute route.
- **Transfer question:** At 04:30, what safe option is already prepared?

## Run of show

| Time | Slide | Presenter move | Expected room response | Transition |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 01 | Pause, then take three rapid options. Do not ask for personal error stories. | Coffee, slow down, check, break, relief, or handoff. | “Which of those options is resourced and authorised?” |
| 1:05 | 02 | Name four performance functions without claiming a universal threshold. | Fatigue is understood as more than a feeling. | “An observational cohort makes the association concrete.” |
| 2:00 | 03 | Read people, reports and one odds ratio. Say associated and self-reported. | Learners see evidence without assuming causation. | “Now test the obvious intervention.” |
| 3:10 | 04 | Ask where the work moves when a shift boundary changes. | Workload, handoffs, coverage and sequence become visible. | “A randomized trial lets us inspect that displacement.” |
| 4:05 | 05 | Reveal workload first, then the observed error rates. | Surprise, followed by recognition that schedule and capacity interact. | “So intervene, then inspect the rest of the system.” |
| 5:25 | 06 | Ask where to intervene and what that control could worsen. | Staffing, recovery, task and resilience controls are connected. | “The system must also allow people to report the hazard.” |
| 6:35 | 07 | Give ten seconds to find one barrier and one enabler. | Organisational and social reporting conditions emerge. | “Now design a response that can actually operate.” |
| 7:35 | 08 | Assign five control families, give 45 seconds and take two reports. | Controls include capacity, detection, adaptation, recovery and learning. | “A response requires more than personal motivation.” |
| 9:00 | 09 | Name coverage, authority and information as control requirements. | Handoff and relief become designed safety work. | “Return to the same night-shift task.” |
| 9:35 | 10 | Deliver the closing line and pause. | No additional discussion needed. | End.

## Interaction facilitation

Give each table one family:

1. **Prevent:** scheduling, workload and recovery opportunity.
2. **Detect:** recognizing fatigue-related risk before a critical task.
3. **Adapt:** altering the task, pace, supervision or interface.
4. **Recover:** intercepting or containing a use error before harm.
5. **Learn:** reporting, measurement and redesign without blame.

Prompt: “It is 04:30 and a high-consequence medication task cannot disappear. Propose one control at your layer. State what authority, staffing or information it needs.”

If the answer is coffee, sleep better, or be careful, ask which upstream condition remains unchanged. Personal strategies can remain minor layers, but cannot create staffing capacity or erase excessive workload.

## Source and claim map

| Slides | Claim or artifact | Source | Boundary that must travel with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 02 | Fatigue can slow reaction time, reduce attention and short-term memory, and impair judgment. | NIOSH, *Fatigue and Work*, updated 2026. | General occupational effects, not a fixed healthcare error rate or universal hour threshold. |
| 03 | 4,826 PGY2+ residents submitted 38,702 monthly reports. Over 60 to 70 hours versus 48 or fewer was associated with self-reported medical error, OR 2.36, 95% CI 2.01 to 2.78. | Weaver et al., *BMJ Medicine*, 2023. | Prospective observational US cohort with self-reported hours and outcomes. Association is not causation. Do not generalize to the whole workforce. |
| 04, 05 | In six pediatric ICUs, the schedule without 24-hour shifts had 8.8 versus 6.7 patients per resident. Resident-related serious errors were observed at 97.1 versus 79.0 per 1,000 patient-days. | Landrigan et al., ROSTERS, *NEJM*, 2020. | Randomized crossover, but site effects varied and workload changed. Secondary workload models were exploratory. The study does not show long shifts are safer. |
| 06 | Evidence on fatigue interventions has mixed effects and is concentrated in physician trainees and scheduling interventions. | AHRQ, *Making Healthcare Safer IV*, 2024. | Rapid review of English-language research since 2013. No universal best policy is established. |
| 07 | Capability, opportunity, motivation and organisational factors influence fatigue reporting behaviour. | HSSIB, *The impact of staff fatigue on patient safety*, 2025, Figure 1. | Reporting-behaviour map, not a causal model or prevalence estimate for fatigue-related harm. |
| 08 to 10 | Control hierarchy and prepared-response design. | Teaching synthesis from the sources above. | Design proposals, not evidence that one control will work without capacity, authority and local evaluation. |

## Claims to refuse

- “Fatigue causes a fixed percentage of medical errors.”
- “Fatigue, sleepiness and burnout are the same.”
- “Every person becomes unsafe after twelve hours.”
- “Duty-hour limits alone improve patient outcomes.”
- “Long shifts are safer because ROSTERS observed fewer errors.”
- “Residents represent nurses, allied health staff and every care setting.”
- “The clinician should simply know their limits.”

Prefer: the system exposed a person to a predictable degraded-performance state while requiring high-consequence work. Self-reporting fatigue is safety behaviour, and the system owes that report a credible response.

## Reuse map

- **Safe six-slide cut:** 01, 02, 03, 05, 08, 10. Budget 6 to 7 minutes.
- **Leadership version:** 01, 04, 05, 06, 09, 10.
- **Must remain together:** 04 and 05; the ROSTERS result and the workload and site-effect boundary; 07 and its reporting-behaviour label.
- **Never separate:** OR 2.36 from self-report, resident-cohort and association language; ROSTERS error rates from the statement that long shifts are not shown to be safer.
- **Can park:** slide 03 if the presentation is about implementation rather than epidemiology; slide 07 in the compact route.

## Final rehearsal checks

1. Say “associated” and “self-reported” on slide 03.
2. Do not imply a universal safe or unsafe shift length.
3. On slide 05, say explicitly that ROSTERS does not show long shifts are safer.
4. On slide 07, say “reporting behaviour,” not “fatigue causation.”
5. End on a prepared system option, not on personal resilience.
