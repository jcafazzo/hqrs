# Presenter guide: Corridor care

## Contract

- **Audience contract:** In ten minutes, learners will identify the care functions that disappear in a temporary environment and design one safeguard with an owner, trigger and failure signal.
- **Argument:** A corridor becomes an underdesigned care system when the patient arrives without reliable ownership, information, medication, monitoring, emergency response, privacy and upstream flow.
- **Runtime:** 10:00 with 40 seconds of pod work and two ten-second reports. Use the twelve-minute route if the call-bell vote is debated.
- **Transfer question:** Does this control reduce harm tonight, reduce the need tomorrow, or do both?

## Run of show

| Time | Slide | Presenter move | Expected room response | Transition |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 01 | Give twelve silent seconds. Ask what care functions are visible and absent. | Equipment, privacy, sightlines, call system, ownership and digital location emerge. | “Temporary can describe intent without describing duration.” |
| 0:50 | 02 | State the 52-hour and 44-hour case facts. | The corridor is experienced as prolonged care, not momentary overflow. | “Now look at one care process inside those hours.” |
| 1:40 | 03 | Reveal 18, 7, 3 and potentially 8. Ask who failed. | Initial person-level answers, followed by wider system conditions. | “A stretcher did not bring the care system with it.” |
| 2:35 | 04 | Name the missing functions around the generated scene. | Learners see workarounds as adaptation to mismatch. | “The mismatch is produced upstream.” |
| 3:30 | 05 | Trace the whole flow path. Ask where ED staff can and cannot intervene. | The corridor becomes a downstream symptom, not a local preference. | “A prevalence snapshot makes the exposure visible.” |
| 4:30 | 06 | Read the denominator and cross-sectional status. | Scale without a claim of individual harm. | “Now inspect one immediate adaptation.” |
| 5:35 | 07 | Vote on whether a call bell normalizes corridor care. | Productive split between mitigation and normalisation concerns. | “We need a control that can operate tonight.” |
| 6:45 | 08 | Assign eight domains, give 40 seconds and take two reports. | Answers name an owner, trigger and failure signal. | “Operations must see the temporary environment as a tracked exposure.” |
| 8:05 | 09 | Describe location, duration, acuity, owner and exit as an operational minimum. | Links bedside adaptation to hospital-wide flow. | “That produces two simultaneous duties.” |
| 9:10 | 10 | Deliver both duties and pause on the final question. | No additional discussion required. | End.

## Interaction facilitation

Assign one domain to each pod of five or six:

1. monitoring and deterioration;
2. time-critical medication;
3. emergency response and call system;
4. staffing, competence and supervision;
5. information, handoff and location tracking;
6. dignity, privacy, nutrition and toileting;
7. patient selection and exclusion criteria;
8. patient flow across hospital and community.

Prompt: “It is 19:00. You cannot create a new inpatient bed tonight. Design one safeguard that reduces immediate risk without pretending the corridor is acceptable. Name the owner, trigger and failure signal.”

Strong answers include an explicit temporary-area owner; location codes in observation systems; a nominated supervisor; time-critical medication reconciliation and access; fixed call capability; exclusion and reassessment criteria; duration tracking; and hospital-wide flow escalation.

## Source and claim map

| Slides | Claim or artifact | Source | Boundary that must travel with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01, 04, 05, 07 | Temporary spaces can lack care functions and hospitals have developed local adaptations. | HSSIB, *Patient care in temporary care environments*, 2026. | Rapid, bounded investigation. Photographs document a setting or adaptation, not patient-level harm or national prevalence. |
| 02, 03 | One 85-year-old man spent 52 hours in the ED, 44 in a corridor. Eighteen capsule-level doses were due; seven were not given, three were late, and potentially eight were on time. | HSSIB, *Medication not given*, 2024. | One investigated event. The later death was not established as caused by corridor care or the medication timing. This is the same event used in the Parkinson module, not an independent case. |
| 06 | 17.7% of 10,042 ED patient observations were in escalation areas across five March 2025 snapshots in 165 UK EDs. Between 54.5% and 61.1% of escalation-area observations were in non-clinical areas. | TERN, *Emergency Medicine Journal*, 2026, DOI 10.1136/emermed-2025-215301. | Cross-sectional observations, not annual incidence, duration, Canadian prevalence, unique-patient count, or causal outcome evidence. |
| 08 to 10 | Safeguard and operational-tracking proposals. | Teaching synthesis from HSSIB risks and adaptations. | Design proposals, not evidence that every site has these controls or that they will eliminate corridor use. |

## Claims to refuse

- “Corridor care caused this patient’s death.”
- “17.7% is the Canadian corridor-care rate.”
- “Every long ED stay is a corridor stay.”
- “A 1:4 or 1:6 staffing ratio is a universal safe standard.”
- “Installing call bells solves corridor care.”
- “ED staff can end corridor use by deciding to stop.”
- “Staff workarounds are automatically unsafe deviance.”

Prefer: staff are often balancing distributed risks with incomplete control. Immediate mitigation and upstream demand reduction remain simultaneous responsibilities.

## Reuse map

- **Safe six-slide cut:** 01, 02, 03, 04, 08, 10. Budget 5 to 6 minutes.
- **Operations version:** 01, 04, 05, 06, 07, 09, 10.
- **Must remain together:** 02 and 03; 06 and its snapshot qualifier; 07 and the phrase “harm reduction, not normalization.”
- **Never separate:** the medication counts from the single-event and no-causal-harm boundary.
- **Do not present beside the Parkinson module as a second case:** state explicitly that both modules use the same investigated event through different lenses.
- **Can park:** either the point-prevalence slide or the call-bell artifact in a shorter presentation, but keep at least one authentic source artifact.

## Classroom and public-use boundary

- Classroom use may show the HSSIB images at full projection scale with report title, date and link.
- Confirm HSSIB image reuse terms before public web publication. If permission is uncertain, retain the source link and replace the photograph with a clearly labelled original conceptual image.
- Keep the source artifact whole enough that the environment remains interpretable. Do not crop it into an emotionally dramatic patient image.
- Generated images must retain visible truth labels and this ledger when copied.

## Final rehearsal checks

1. State that corridor and Parkinson modules share one event.
2. Say “potentially eight on time” on slide 03.
3. Say “five snapshots” and “patient observations” on slide 06.
4. State that a call bell is mitigation, not a solution to patient flow.
5. Stop after two pod reports on the ten-minute route, or four on the twelve-minute route, and close on the two duties.
