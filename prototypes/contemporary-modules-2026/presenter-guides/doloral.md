# Presenter guide: Doloral

Status: READY · library acceptance passed 19 July 2026
Entry point: `../doloral.html`
Target: 8 to 10 minutes, ten slides

## Audience contract and argument

**Contract:** Notice what the display invites you to perceive before deciding who should have looked harder.

**Argument:** The use error emerged where product naming, data fields, display layout and ordinary visual grouping met.

The module is a Canadian object lesson in semantic coupling. It also shows a completed learning loop: reporting made a pattern visible and contributed to a change in the product name at the source.

## Structured interaction

Use five tables of roughly nine learners:

1. Manufacturer and naming
2. Regulator and database
3. Software and display
4. Clinical organization
5. Learning system

Prompt:

> You own one layer. Propose one control that does not depend on perfect attention. Then name one way your control could silently fail downstream.

Expected responses include a naming review that tests name and strength together, labelled display columns, a delimiter that survives responsive layouts and print, automated detection of a name-ending digit beside a strength-leading digit, end-to-end display tests using the live terminology feed, pharmacy review of new formulary items, and incident aggregation that can reach upstream owners.

The point is not to identify one perfect control. Ask which control still works when a clinician is interrupted, the screen is small and a downstream export changes the layout.

## Caveats and forbidden shortcuts

- Do not say 11-fold overdose. The bulletin says 11-fold dosing errors and does not state direction.
- Do not state an exact incident or patient count.
- Do not invent the severe harm, treatment, recovery or outcome.
- Do not call this a typo, spelling error, database corruption or wrong-drug selection.
- Do not call Figure 1 an authentic production screenshot.
- Do not assign sole blame to a hospital, vendor, clinician or regulator.
- Do not say Health Canada ordered a recall or withdrew the product.
- Do not say the rename eliminated all downstream risk.
- Do not imply a warning alert was the documented solution.
- Incident reports identify a signal and pattern, not a denominator or general display-error rate.

## Reuse rules

**Five-minute cut:** use slides 1, 3, 4, 8, 9 and 10. Narrate the perceptual principle and learning loop without the table exercise. If even shorter, use slides 1, 3, 8 and 10, but keep every evidence qualifier.

**Must remain together:**

- Slide 1 must retain the re-creation label and direct source.
- Slide 3 must retain "several," "11-fold dosing errors," "at least one" and the line stating what was not reported.
- Slide 8 must not be shown as a recall or withdrawal.
- Slide 9 must show both strength and current marketed status and retain the downstream-refresh caveat.

**Never separate:** Do not copy "11×" without "dosing errors" and the direction-unknown caveat. Do not copy DOLORAL SIROP without a strength. Do not use generated generic objects as photographs of the product.

For public publication, preserve source links and figure attribution. Fair dealing for a classroom does not automatically authorize public-web republication. If source-image terms are uncertain, replace the embedded source figure with a direct linked observation prompt.
