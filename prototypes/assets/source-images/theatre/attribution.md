# Systems Theatre source-image attributions

These assets are retained for attributed, noncommercial teaching use in the HQRS 846 prototype. Keep the source credit visible on the audience-facing slide or in an adjacent persistent source footer. Do not use the images to imply that the cited investigation established a causal effect beyond its stated findings.

## Epic Sepsis Model v2

### `esm-v2-figure-1-performance.jpg`

- **Source/owner:** Wong A, Currey D, Schwinne M, et al.; *JAMA Network Open* / American Medical Association. The article is distributed under CC BY.
- **Article:** “Multicenter Prospective Validation of an Updated Proprietary Sepsis Prediction Model” (2026), DOI `10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.0181`.
- **URL:** https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2845595
- **Repository image:** https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/45a2/12949446/ddb3a19a2091/jamanetwopen-e260181-g001.jpg
- **Designation:** Figure 1, “Encounter-Level Discriminative Performance of the Epic Sepsis Model Version 2 Across Study Sites.”
- **Suggested educational use:** Project large and crop to one or two panels while asking students what changes across institutions. This is the strongest visual for threshold-dependent variation in sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** This is one ESM v2 product family, not one identical fitted model. At all four study sites, ESM v2 was fine-tuned before implementation using a **site-specific historical training set of adult inpatients**. Site thresholds and clinical workflows were also locally configured.
- **Suggested slide credit:** `Wong et al., JAMA Network Open, 2026 · Fig. 1 · CC BY`

### `esm-v2-figure-2-v1-comparison.jpg`

- **Source/owner:** Wong A, Currey D, Schwinne M, et al.; *JAMA Network Open* / American Medical Association. The article is distributed under CC BY.
- **Article:** “Multicenter Prospective Validation of an Updated Proprietary Sepsis Prediction Model” (2026), DOI `10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.0181`.
- **URL:** https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2845595
- **Repository image:** https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/45a2/12949446/5033c138f12e/jamanetwopen-e260181-g002.jpg
- **Designation:** Figure 2, “Dot Plot Showing Comparison of Epic Sepsis Model Version 2 and Version 1 Performance Before Sepsis-3 Positivity and Clinician Recognition.”
- **Suggested educational use:** Use as the evidence reveal after learners see the four health systems. The paired panels make it possible to discuss both improvement over ESM v1 and the remaining between-site variation.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** Do not caption this “the same model performed differently in four hospitals.” The study involved four health systems; Emory contributed encounters from six affiliated hospitals. All four sites used the ESM v2 product family, but each used a locally fine-tuned model. The study was prognostic and did not evaluate effects on mortality or other clinical outcomes.
- **Suggested slide credit:** `Wong et al., JAMA Network Open, 2026 · Fig. 2 · CC BY`

## Temporary care environments

### `hssib-tce-figure-1-corridor.jpg`

- **Source/owner:** Health Services Safety Investigations Body (HSSIB).
- **Report:** “Patient care in temporary care environments,” published 8 January 2026.
- **URL:** https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/patient-care-in-temporary-care-environments/investigation-report/
- **Original image:** https://hssib-ovd42x6f-media.s3.amazonaws.com/production-assets/images/Figure_1_TCE.max-1200x1200.jpg
- **Designation:** Figure 1, “Patients on trolleys in a corridor being used as a temporary care environment due to overflow in the emergency department.”
- **Suggested educational use:** Use as a full-bleed documentary opening for Station A. Invite learners to inventory system conditions visible or implied by the environment before adding labels such as sightlines, monitoring, staffing, privacy, call bells, oxygen/suction, and flow.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** The report did not find evidence that the temporary environment or emergency response affected the outcome of the reported patient collapse. Treat the photograph as evidence of the care setting, not proof of a particular harm.
- **Suggested slide credit:** `HSSIB, Patient care in temporary care environments, 2026 · Fig. 1`

### `hssib-tce-figure-4-call-bell.jpg`

- **Source/owner:** Health Services Safety Investigations Body (HSSIB).
- **Report:** “Patient care in temporary care environments,” published 8 January 2026.
- **URL:** https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/patient-care-in-temporary-care-environments/investigation-report/
- **Original image:** https://hssib-ovd42x6f-media.s3.amazonaws.com/production-assets/images/Figure_4_TCE.max-1200x1200.jpg
- **Designation:** Figure 4, “Example of an adapted temporary care environment in a corridor in an emergency department, with an electric point and emergency call bell.”
- **Suggested educational use:** Pair with Figure 1 as a reality/redesign diptych. Ask which changes alter the physical system and which merely ask staff to compensate. The wall-mounted call bell, electrical points, oxygen equipment, and numbered spaces make the comparison concrete.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** Present this as an observed adaptation, not a validated intervention or endorsement of corridor care. HSSIB explicitly describes tension between avoiding normalisation and mitigating risks where temporary environments are already in use.
- **Suggested slide credit:** `HSSIB, Patient care in temporary care environments, 2026 · Fig. 4`

## Staff fatigue

### `hssib-fatigue-figure-1-reporting.png`

- **Source/owner:** Health Services Safety Investigations Body (HSSIB), adapted for accessibility from Shorrock (2023).
- **Report:** “The impact of staff fatigue on patient safety,” published 24 April 2025.
- **URL:** https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/the-impact-of-staff-fatigue-on-patient-safety/investigation-report/
- **Original image:** https://hssib-ovd42x6f-media.s3.amazonaws.com/production-assets/images/Fig_1_Fatigue_YgceD30.max-1200x1200.png
- **Designation:** Figure 1, “Factors that influence reporting behaviour.”
- **Suggested educational use:** Reveal the network after asking why fatigue is rarely captured in incident-learning systems. Crop or spotlight the three bold nodes—capability, opportunity, and motivation—before revealing the surrounding organizational conditions.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** This figure explains influences on **reporting behaviour**; it is not a causal model of fatigue-related harm. HSSIB found that the scale of fatigue-related patient-safety harm was poorly measured, and the pregnancy-scan account in the report was a staff member’s belief rather than independent causal adjudication.
- **Suggested slide credit:** `HSSIB, The impact of staff fatigue on patient safety, 2025 · Fig. 1 · adapted from Shorrock (2023)`

## Automated dispensing cabinet deployment

### `ismp-adc-figure-1-frameshift.jpg`

- **Source/owner:** Institute for Safe Medication Practices Canada (ISMP Canada).
- **Bulletin:** “Emergency Preparedness Mitigates Patient Harm during Failed Deployment of Upgraded Automated Dispensing Cabinets,” Volume 25, Issue 8, published 31 July 2025.
- **URL:** https://ismpcanada.ca/bulletin/automated-dispensing-cabinets/
- **Original image:** https://ismpcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025i8f1-ADC-frameshift-error.jpg
- **Designation:** Figure 1, “Simplified example of a frameshift error of data importation to an automated dispensing cabinet (ADC).”
- **Suggested educational use:** Show the original table first, then the shifted table. Ask learners to locate where the error was created and why the displayed values remained superficially plausible enough to propagate.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** This is a simplified explanatory diagram, not a screenshot of the production ADC. ISMP Canada reports that the actual import error misaligned multiple fields and affected more than 300 medication products and more than 150 transactions.
- **Suggested slide credit:** `ISMP Canada Safety Bulletin, 2025 · Vol. 25, Issue 8 · Fig. 1`

### `ismp-adc-figure-2-deployment.jpg`

- **Source/owner:** Institute for Safe Medication Practices Canada (ISMP Canada).
- **Bulletin:** “Emergency Preparedness Mitigates Patient Harm during Failed Deployment of Upgraded Automated Dispensing Cabinets,” Volume 25, Issue 8, published 31 July 2025.
- **URL:** https://ismpcanada.ca/bulletin/automated-dispensing-cabinets/
- **Original image:** https://ismpcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025i8f12-ADC-deployment.jpg
- **Designation:** Figure 2, “Overview of the key steps involved in deployment of an upgraded ADC.”
- **Suggested educational use:** Use after the resilience reveal to move from incident reconstruction to system design: readiness, escalation, testing, communication, supported deployment, debriefing, and monitoring.
- **Accuracy guardrail:** No patient harm was **reported** in the described incident. Preserve “reported.” The successful recovery depended on nurses’ reports, pharmacy response, technical expertise, an emergency preparedness plan, and activation of the emergency operations centre; it was not evidence that the deployment process was intrinsically safe.
- **Suggested slide credit:** `ISMP Canada Safety Bulletin, 2025 · Vol. 25, Issue 8 · Fig. 2`

## Recommended sequencing in Systems Theatre

1. Use `esm-v2-figure-1-performance.jpg` as the main AI evidence reveal; keep Figure 2 available for a subsequent comparison or appendix state.
2. Use the two HSSIB corridor photographs as a matched pair: **care setting as found** → **physical adaptations observed**.
3. Use the HSSIB fatigue figure only after the class has proposed person-level explanations; its density works as the visual consequence of widening the causal frame.
4. Use the ISMP frameshift diagram to show propagation and the deployment arrow to show anticipation/recovery. Together they preserve the case’s central point: people interrupted a technical failure rather than causing patient harm.
