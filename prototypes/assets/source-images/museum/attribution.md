# Museum of Designed Error — source-image attributions

These files are excerpts from the cited primary sources. They are intended for a non-commercial graduate-course lecture that critiques how device, interface, deployment, and work-system design shape safety. Keep a compact source credit visible on every slide that uses an image, and retain the full citation in presenter notes or the course site.

## Important limitation

The landmark Sjoding et al. article remains the recommended evidence source: Michael W. Sjoding et al., [“Racial Bias in Pulse Oximetry Measurement”](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc2029240), *New England Journal of Medicine* 383 (2020): 2477–2478, DOI 10.1056/NEJMc2029240. Its Figure 1 was **not** placed in this folder because both the publisher and the PMC author-manuscript page returned automated-access security challenges during retrieval. A third-party mirror was deliberately not used. If the instructor downloads the figure or article PDF through institutional access, Figure 1 is the best visual to add: it directly compares pulse-oximetry and arterial saturation by race and shows the occult-hypoxemia region.

The FDA excerpts are current primary-source regulatory material and work well as the redesign response, but they do not substitute for Sjoding et al.'s empirical Figure 1. Also retain the FDA's visible `Draft – Not for Implementation` label; do not present the document as final guidance.
