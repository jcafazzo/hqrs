Bottom line

Strong theatre. Weak transfer.

The legacy deck makes attention demonstrations memorable, but the system-design conclusion arrives only at the end. The rebuild follows every mechanism with a clinical transfer and a design response.

What remains

CT expert search, low-prevalence detection, the fluorouracil label, the fallibility premise and Jobs-style hard cuts.

What changes

The live job becomes: diagnose what shaped detection, memory and judgment—and redesign the conditions for recovery.

Learner transformation

Replace “Who failed to pay attention?” with “What shaped attention, memory and judgment here—and what should the system make easier to detect, remember, challenge or recover from?”

Independent reviewer lenses

Adversarial, because the subject deserves precision.

Cognitive science

  • The deck conflates change blindness and inattentional blindness.
  • Low-prevalence search is a signal-detection problem, not simply availability bias.
  • Working memory is limited and task-dependent—not a universal four-item bin.
  • “Posterior probability bias” is opaque; teach diagnostic momentum in plain language.

Systems safety

  • Bias labels are descriptions, not root causes.
  • After every phenomenon, say “therefore the system must…”
  • Preference and performance are both evidence.
  • Higher-leverage controls still require local testing and monitoring.

Mixed graduate cohort

  • The old deck is physician- and visual-diagnosis-heavy.
  • Use nursing, pharmacy, allied-health, patient and operational work.
  • Replace gotchas with predict → commit → reveal → normalize → redesign.
  • Psychological safety is part of the pedagogy.

Equity and stagecraft

  • A one-line “in-group bias” slide is too thin for a structural problem.
  • The urinal sequence is gender-narrow and vastly overlong.
  • Long book excerpts and screenshots are unreadable from the back.
  • Observation evidence gets its own full-field slide.

Claim audit

Precision before polish.

20 of 24

Say “20 of 24 radiologists in one experimental nodule-search task,” not a general 83% clinical miss rate. Drew et al.

8 of 15

The cited door-swap field experiment reported eight of fifteen failing to report the change—not two-thirds. Simons and Levin

No fixed bin

Working-memory performance varies with chunking, expertise, interference and task structure. Cowan review

Fatigue ≠ intoxication slogan

Task-specific laboratory equivalence does not justify an unqualified legal-intoxication claim. Dawson and Reid

Framing mismatch

Do not attach published 72%/78% results to a modified Canadian avian-flu vignette. Tversky and Kahneman

Nudge provenance

The famous 80% urinal-spillage reduction is widely repeated but weakly documented. 2025 provenance discussion

Biased AI changes the team

In a 2023 randomized vignette study, systematically biased AI reduced accuracy by 11.3 percentage points; image explanations did not meaningfully rescue performance. Teach workflow design, not “AI versus doctors.”

Secure-chat concurrency

A 2025 observational cohort of 3,232 physicians and advanced practice providers across 50,027 clinician-days at 14 US hospitals associated four or more concurrent conversations with 54.8 additional EHR minutes and 41.6 additional chart switches. It demonstrates cognitive work, not causation of error.
