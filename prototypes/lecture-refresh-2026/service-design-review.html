Bottom line

The blueprint must become an operational commitment.

The redesign moves learners from “improve the experience” to naming the people, information, capacity, authority, exception paths, implementation conditions and measures that make a care promise real.

What remains

Shostack provenance, blueprint anatomy, field methods, Medly’s authentic touchpoints and service maps, and the live workshop.

What changes

Martha’s Rule opens the lecture. Equity, recovery, workload transfer and implementation become blueprint lanes rather than footnotes.

Learner transformation

A healthcare service is a sociotechnical promise delivered across people, workflows, organizations, technologies and policy. I can map it, expose who bears the work and risk, and define how it will be implemented, measured and sustained.

Independent reviewer lenses

The hidden work is the course content.

Service design

  • Pages 4–60 spend too long proving that products sit inside services.
  • Apple market value does not establish service-design causation.
  • The blueprint should be taught as a testable hypothesis, not a picture.
  • Happy paths conceal the service; exception paths reveal it.

Implementation science

  • The service and the strategy used to implement it are different design objects.
  • Readiness, staffing, governance, resources, cost and sustainment are missing.
  • Reach, adoption, implementation and maintenance are a useful realism test.
  • Future-state changes need feedback and de-implementation criteria.

Equity and power

  • “Customer” is too narrow for multi-sided health services.
  • Consultation is not co-design; shared authority, recognition and compensation matter.
  • Every convenience transfers work to someone.
  • Newcomer material needs ethical reframing and public-consent review.

Operations and stagecraft

  • Expose demand, capacity, queues, handoff ownership, downtime and failure demand.
  • Backstage includes policy, finance, procurement, workforce and data governance.
  • Blueprints and maps must be full-field, not framed miniatures.
  • Patient testimony is voice—not outcome evidence.

Choose a portfolio, not a pile.

Martha’s Rule

A poster and phone number become a system of awareness, trust, rapid review, authority, capacity and follow-through. Current management data through April 2026 support a powerful—but carefully caveated—opening.

Corridor care

Virtual wards / hospital at home

New concepts

What the old blueprint does not show.

Service debt

Unowned handoffs, shadow queues and manual workarounds accumulate like technical debt.

Work transfer

Every convenience moves cognitive, emotional, administrative, travel or data work.

Power mapping

Who can escalate, approve an exception, access information or stop unsafe work?

Exception path

No device. No English. Night shift. Downtime. Rejected referral. High demand.

Three outcome layers

Implementation, service, and health/equity outcomes need different measures.

Blueprint as hypothesis

Observation and operational data must validate how work and value actually move.
