# Tricolour Monument

Tricolour Monument treats the Queen’s palette as architecture, not decoration. Queen’s Blue is the stage: calm, deep, and authoritative enough to carry a room. Red and Gold become spatial cuts through that field—large, unmistakable blocks that make the brand visible from the back row without turning the slide into a marketing template.

The teaching slide uses one idea at Jobs-stage scale. The wordmark occupies a protected white masthead so its supplied navy artwork remains unaltered, legible, and surrounded by clear space. The tricolour rule and monumental side bands create a repeatable course signature, while the large title carries the actual teaching moment. There are no cards, ornamental frames, or interface chrome competing with the argument.

Hierarchy comes from contrast and proportion. Open Sans or a system sans does the work with oversized type, tight display tracking, and generous line-height in the supporting sentence. Gold marks the conceptual pivot—“not”—instead of colouring arbitrary words. Red signals urgency and reframing; Blue holds the institutional voice; White gives the identity system room to breathe.

The observation slide follows a different rule: when students must inspect evidence, the source becomes the slide. The HSSIB diagram is expanded to the full available width on a white field without a card or inset border. A large “OBSERVE” cue tells the room to stop listening and look, while a concise attribution preserves provenance without shrinking the evidence.

This direction is intentionally more ceremonial than the existing prototypes. Its strength is immediate Queen’s recognition and theatrical scale. Its tradeoff is that the strong colour blocks can become repetitive across a long deck, so the system should alternate monument slides with quieter full-field evidence, photography, and warm-white discussion moments rather than stamp every slide with identical geometry.
