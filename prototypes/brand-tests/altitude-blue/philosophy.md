# Altitude Blue

Altitude Blue treats a lecture as a landscape that the audience moves through, not a sequence of decorated cards. Queen’s Blue is the atmosphere and the structural line: it carries headlines, paths, and the deepest visual field. Gold is a moment of attention and Red is a moment of consequence. Their scarcity makes them legible from the back row.

The teaching slide uses sculptural layers of white, warm paper, and pale altitude blue. Those layers suggest that an apparently simple clinical act rests on conditions that rise and fall around it. The fine blue path is continuous until the gold pivot—an almost invisible change in conditions—redirects the story toward the red consequence. The idea is conveyed before the instructor explains it.

Typography is deliberately large, close, and unornamented. Open Sans and the system sans stack feel contemporary and institutional without becoming corporate. The headline is the event; supporting copy is limited to one breath. Queen’s branding sits with generous clear space, while the tricolour appears as function rather than page decoration.

Evidence receives a separate stage. When students must inspect a source, the source fills almost the entire 16:9 field, with no frame, card, or miniature caption competing for attention. A 26-pixel attribution rail remains visible at 1600 × 900, distinguishing the original source from the course’s interpretation while preserving back-row readability.

The tradeoff is purposeful restraint. Altitude Blue gives up the dramatic black-stage energy of the current prototypes and some of the warmth of a full heritage treatment. In return, it offers a pervasive Queen’s identity, a calmer Apple-like rhythm, and a visual grammar that can move cleanly between a declarative teaching moment and a source page that needs to be observed.
