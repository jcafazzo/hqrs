# Collective Revision

**2026 brand layer:** This direction now operates inside [Causal Tricolour](../queens-visual-philosophy.md): Queen’s Blue replaces violet as the stage, Gold marks new information, and Red identifies consequence. Original GPT Image 2 scenes and six full-screen observation states keep the room—not interface chrome—as the protagonist.

Collective Revision treats the frame as a dark stage on which interpretations assemble, conflict, and change. Space begins open and expectant. Nine small units—marks, seats, cards, or voices—form a disciplined chorus around a central event. Their arrangement must be meticulously crafted so the group reads as both many perspectives and one coherent instrument.

Violet carries uncertainty, cyan marks new information, amber records pressure, and coral identifies the first attractive explanation. Colour changes are consequential: they show a belief being revised rather than decorate a transition. Each chromatic shift should be calibrated with painstaking care by someone fluent in projection, contrast, and the emotional weight of light.

Scale moves between room and system. A single prompt can dominate like a stage direction; four hospitals can appear as equal scenic units; one system layer can expand until the earlier attribution no longer fits. Typography is terse and performative—questions, choices, verdicts—not prose. Motion is limited to causal disclosure and must feel rehearsed, exact, and fully under the presenter’s control.

Craft is expressed through timing and spatial memory. Elements return to the places from which they emerged; revisions preserve traces of prior beliefs; the audience can always tell what changed. The visual system should look as if it were refined through countless rehearsals, with every cue, alignment, and pause resolved to master-level precision. The finished artifact is not decorated theatre but an instrument for collective attention.
