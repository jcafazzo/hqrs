# Visual philosophy — Service Design

The deck turns a blueprint from a small diagram into a staged argument. Learners first see the promise, then the frontstage, backstage, information, capacity, authority, exception, implementation, and measurement layers. Each reveal adds one operational obligation.

- Queen's Blue provides the stable system field.
- Gold marks a service promise, ownership pivot, or design opportunity.
- Red marks a failure path, transferred burden, or unsafe exception.
- Whole maps establish context; dedicated close-ups make each layer observable from the back of the room.
- CSS-authored diagrams are working models, not claims of empirical truth.
- Case evidence is separated from testimonial voice and always paired with a limitation.

The visual rhythm retains keynote-style confidence while refusing the polished happy path. A service is judged most clearly where conditions are difficult, demand exceeds capacity, or someone cannot use the expected channel.
